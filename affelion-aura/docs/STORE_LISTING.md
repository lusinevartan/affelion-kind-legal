**Description:**

Aura turns one minute of your body's own signals into a portrait.

Put a fingertip over the back camera. The flash lights your skin, the camera sees each
heartbeat, and Aura measures your pulse and how much it varies from beat to beat. Say a few
words and it measures the pitch and steadiness of your voice. Then it takes your picture.

Those numbers become color. Twenty colors, each with a plain meaning: teal for calm focus, deep
red for a racing pulse, golden yellow for bright energy. The same numbers always give the same
color, and the report shows you which number led to which color.

Then breathe. In the guided practice you keep your finger on the camera and watch the color
change as your heart rhythm settles, with a before and after from the same session.

What you get free: first scan free, then one free scan a week, each with the portrait and the main reading.

Aura Pro adds unlimited scans, the full report, your history and trends, the breathing practice
without limits, Sasha (a guide who explains your portrait using only what was measured), more
portrait styles and clean exports.

What Aura is not. It does not photograph anything invisible. It does not detect energy fields,
predict anything or diagnose anything. It is not a medical device and its readings are not for
medical use.

Private by design. Camera frames and audio are processed on your phone and discarded. Your
portraits and numbers stay on your phone unless you save or share them. No account is needed.

## Notes for App Review

> Aura measures pulse with the standard fingertip method: the user covers the rear camera and
> flash, and the app reads the small brightness change of each heartbeat (photoplethysmography).
> It reports heart rate and beat-to-beat variation only. It does not measure blood pressure,
> blood oxygen, temperature or glucose, and it makes no medical claims; the app and its listing
> say it is not a medical device.
>
> The colors are a visual translation of those measurements using a fixed, published rule set.
> The app states plainly that it does not capture anything invisible.
>
> No account or login is needed. To test: complete onboarding, tap Scan, cover the rear camera
> and flash with a fingertip for about 40 seconds, speak when asked, then hold still for the
> photo. The first scan is free, then one free scan a week. The paywall opens once, two seconds
> after the first portrait, and can be closed with "Not now". Subscriptions are listed on it, and
> the lifetime purchase sits behind "Prefer to pay once?", with Restore, Terms of Use and Privacy Policy links. Sasha, the in-app guide, is an AI assistant
> and says so; in this version her answers are generated on the device from the user's own
> readings, and nothing is sent to a server.
