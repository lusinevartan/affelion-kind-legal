Brand & Website Case Study · Stagecoach · Washington, DC · 2026

Stagecoach

Emergency care that comes to you.

A doctor-led emergency medicine practice in Washington, DC, preparing to open. I built the brand, wrote and built the website, and produced the print and apparel files the team needs to launch.

Pre-launch. The practice is awaiting EMS Agency approval from the District of Columbia, so the site runs a waitlist until then.

The Brief

Doctors who come to you when it is too urgent to wait and not bad enough for 911.

Stagecoach sends physicians and medics to homes, offices and events in Washington, DC. The founders had the team, the vehicles and the clinical model. They needed everything a member or a partner would see.

Two rules shaped all of it. Nothing on the site may read as a substitute for 911, so that line sits in every footer. And no statistic goes on a page unless the practice can source it, so there are none.

The Mark

One S, drawn as two arcs around a dot.

The same shape is the logo, the opening of the website, the step tracker in How It Works, and the embossing on the kit bag.

Four layouts: horizontal, stacked, symbol and wordmark. Five colourways. Every file is outlined artwork in PDF and EPS for print, SVG for screens and transparent PNG at three sizes, so no vendor needs the fonts.

Navy and porcelain carry the identity. Coral appears once per surface: the dot in the S, the one button on a page, a single stitch on the cap.

The Website

Nine pages, written in plain English.

Home, Services, Membership, Kids, Events, Team, Careers, Partners, Contact, and a 404. Built as static pages with one stylesheet and one script.

The S opens the site once per visit as a mask over the team's own footage, then settles into the header. How It Works is a step tracker tied to scroll: call, decide, we come. Coral is reserved for one action, Join the Waitlist.

Checked at 23 viewport sizes for overflow and overlap. Automated accessibility scan with axe-core returned zero violations; keyboard, focus and contrast checked by hand. Every page, link and image verified loading.

Print & Apparel

Print and apparel files, ready for a vendor.

Business cards for ten team members, CMYK with bleed. Tees, quarter-zip and cap with embroidery specs. Shopping bag, tote and a leather kit bag. Letterhead and an email signature.

The kit is 221 files in seven folders with a start-here note, and a 22-page booklet that shows each piece in use so the team can brief a vendor directly.

Handoff

The team edits the site with their own Claude account, without a developer.

The site was imported into the client's Lovable project as plain files. A brief in the repository tells any agent the brand rules, the legal lines and where every file lives; a short guide and a page of ready prompts cover the common changes.

The booklet opens as facing pages with a contents list and bookmarks, so it reads as a book rather than a deck.
