# PGA TOUR — ShotLink Value Proposition: Decision Map & Point of View

**Prepared:** August 18, 2026
**Source:** Team debrief following the ShotLink scoping call (Damien, Kevin, Nate, Raúl, Brad; Lance and client-side context via Damien)
**Status:** Working draft for team validation — numbers and framing to be confirmed before client use

---

## 1. The one-sentence read on this engagement

This is not a typical "grow it, spin it, or sell it" asset review. The sponsors who hired us believe ShotLink is core to the PGA TOUR and need an independent, evidence-based case that a skeptical new executive group will actually believe. The current-state value of the asset — not just its future — is on trial.

As Nate put it on the debrief: normally current-state value isn't at stake and the work is about expansion. Here, the work is a defensive-and-offensive strategy: prove *whether and why* ShotLink is core, then show what it could become.

---

## 2. Core belief statement (draft — validate against the formal brief)

> By the end of this engagement, PGA TOUR's new executive group should believe that ShotLink is not a product line to be judged on its direct P&L, but operating infrastructure woven through the TOUR's business — scheduling, purses and payouts, licensing, betting and gambling, broadcast and partner ecosystems — whose value must be measured the way infrastructure is measured; and that EY is the partner who can prove that value independently, quantify the growth left on the table, and give leadership a defensible answer either way.

The "either way" matters. Our credibility with the executive group — and our protection against the sponsor-bias risk Raúl flagged — comes from being visibly willing to conclude that parts of the investment don't pay back.

**The core communication insight:** the value story didn't get worse — the audience changed. ShotLink's advocates are still pitching in the encoding that worked for an extroverted, externally facing CEO. The new CEO decides analytically. Our job is to re-encode, not just re-argue.

**Competitive positioning:** Accenture or Deloitte would bring a tech-focused view. Our position is the holistic business view — technical understanding integrated with strategic value — delivered with the "hygiene" and structure an analytical leadership team requires, while guiding the journey rather than overanalyzing every detail.

## 5. Board and new-CEO communication approach (draft POV)

1. **Answer the CEO's questions in the CEO's order.** Lead with the decision architecture — what was spent, what it returns, what breaks without it, what the options are — not the product story. An analytical leader wants to see the reasoning before the conclusion.
2. **Pre-read, then discuss.** Match the introvert's processing style: send the analysis ahead, let the CEO digest privately, and use the meeting for interrogation of assumptions rather than presentation theater.
3. **A value ledger, not a narrative deck.** Every claim traceable to a source; assumptions explicit; ranges rather than point estimates (essential given the unreconciled $100M vs. $800M attributable-revenue figures). Auditable beats persuasive with this audience.
4. **Run the subtraction test.** "What breaks without ShotLink" — scheduling, purses and payouts, betting integrity, broadcast, licensing — is more convincing to an analytical mind than any growth story. Counterfactual value is the natural language of this CEO.
5. **Answer the Ferrari question head-on.** Include a genuine build/buy/partner/descope comparison — "will something else do?" — rather than defending around it. Visibly entertaining the kill option is what makes the rest of the case credible, and it is what Lance is watching for.
6. **One fact base, two encodings.** The outgoing, externally facing narrative still matters — for partners, media, and the street. Build a single source of truth that renders two ways: the analytical ledger for the CEO and board, the story for external audiences. This bridges the two leadership dispositions instead of choosing between them.
7. **Layer the depth; don't overanalyze.** One-page decision summary → model → appendix. Consistent metrics presented the same way each time so the board builds pattern recognition. Rigor visible at the summary level, drill-down available on demand — hygiene and structure without drowning the room.

This communication design is itself the differentiation: a tech-focused competitor delivers an assessment; we deliver a decision system tuned to how this specific CEO and board actually decide.

## 6. What we actually heard (signal, not just notes)

- **Inclusive language, partner-friendly:** they've used partners in bespoke ways before and talk like collaborators. Low friction to work with; also means they'll expect us inside the tent quickly.
- **The tell:** asked two product questions, "we couldn't stop them." The passion is real — the transmission of that passion to the new executives is what's broken. Our job is as much translation as valuation.
- **"No pride in ownership, if they kill it I don't care"** — said, and clearly not true. Read it as permission to be honest, not as indifference.
- **The asset is being "sweated" literally and figuratively:** deeply woven into the PGA TOUR's workings — scheduling, payouts and purses, betting/gambling, licensing pass-throughs — with maybe handfuls of revenue streams rather than a couple.
- **Money is not the constraint** (they bought a golf cart company and then a trucking company to move the carts). The constraint is strategic clarity and internal belief.
- **They told us how to win the work:** understand the technology far better than our current articulation shows, within a short upfront phase. That is an evaluation criterion, stated out loud.

## 8. Draft phase arc (per Brad's framing, for confirmation)

1. **Current state + market screening** — technology, IP, and adjacencies included; establish the audited fact base.
2. **Options** — expansion, partnership, restructuring, monetization; explicitly include the honest "descope" branch.
3. **Business case** — value proposition to the PGA TOUR and its potential partners, with the direct/indirect ledger.
4. **Board presentation** — the recommended strategy, and the internal brand elevation of ShotLink: "this is the value ShotLink is offering, this is the growth profile, these are the strategies considered, this is the best."
